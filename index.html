Core idea

Scarcity → choice

Limited resources force people and societies to choose. Every choice creates a trade-off.

Core idea

Opportunity cost

The true economic cost of a choice is the value of the next-best alternative you give up.

Core idea

Equilibrium

Markets coordinate buyers and sellers through prices. Equilibrium is where planned buying equals planned selling.

The Ten Principles — compressed mental map

exam-ready
1. Trade-offs

Getting more of one objective usually means giving up something else.

2. Opportunity cost

The cost of a choice is what you give up to get it.

3. Think at the margin

Compare additional benefit with additional cost.

4. Incentives matter

People react when costs or rewards change.

5. Trade can help

Specialization can make both sides better off.

6. Markets coordinate

Prices guide decentralized decisions.

7. Government can improve outcomes

Especially with market failure or distribution goals.

8. Productivity drives living standards

More output per worker supports higher real incomes.

9. Too much money → inflation

Persistent money growth can create sustained price increases.

10. Short-run inflation–unemployment trade-off

Aggregate demand changes may temporarily affect both.

Positive vs Normative

Positive statement

Describes how the world works and can, in principle, be tested with evidence.

“A higher minimum wage reduces employment for some workers.”
Normative statement

Expresses a value judgment about how the world should be.

“The government should raise the minimum wage.”
Chapter 3

Comparative Advantage & Gains from Trade

The key distinction: absolute advantage asks who is more productive; comparative advantage asks who gives up less of something else.
